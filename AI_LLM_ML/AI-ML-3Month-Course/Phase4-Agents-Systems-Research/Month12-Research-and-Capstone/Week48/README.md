# Week 48 — Final Capstone: Validate, Write, Present

The analysis note week: in physics a result doesn't exist until it survives review — this week your capstone gets the validation, writeup, demo, and talk that make it real.

## Objectives

- Run the complete pre-committed evaluation plan and report every number, favorable or not.
- Stress the system beyond the happy path: track-appropriate robustness checks (adversarial questions, out-of-distribution conditions, seed variation, ablations).
- Write the capstone report with an architecture diagram, results against all baselines, and a limitations section written to be believed.
- Record a working demo and a 15–20 minute mock research talk, delivered as if to a lab ML seminar.
- Close the course: verify the End-Phase-4 gate and the sign-off protocol.

## Core material (~3 hrs)

- Re-read `PROPOSAL.md` and Week 46's writing notes — the writeup is graded against the proposal's own success criteria.
- Track-specific validation reference: (a) your Week-40 agent-eval harness; (b) CaloChallenge or your pre-committed distributional metrics; (c) your Week-40 trajectory-review method; (d) the target paper's own evaluation protocol; (e) CI evals + latency/cost + injection tests.
- One talk-craft resource by title (e.g. the "How to give a great research talk" companion to Week 46's writing guide) — structure, one-point-per-slide, ending with limitations.

## Exercises (built when the week starts)

1. Final evaluation: full eval plan on the frozen system, multiple seeds where stochastic. Accept when: results table reports every pre-committed metric with variation across seeds/runs, generated by one command.
2. Stress suite: ≥5 track-appropriate stress tests (e.g. copilot: ambiguous and adversarial analysis questions; fast-sim: energies/angles at the edge of the training range; repro-extend: sensitivity to the guessed hyperparameters). Accept when: `stress.md` reports each test's outcome, including the failures.
3. Ablation: remove or degrade one core component and re-run the headline metric. Accept when: the ablation row is in the results table and one sentence states what it shows the component contributes.
4. Writeup: 6–10 page report — intro, architecture diagram, method, results vs. baselines, limitations, future work. Accept when: every claim cites a table/figure, and the limitations section names ≥3 real weaknesses a referee would find anyway.
5. Demo: recorded screen capture, ≤10 minutes, fresh clone → `uv sync` → one command → the headline capability, narrated. Accept when: the recording shows the clean-clone run, not a warm environment.
6. Mock talk: 15–20 minutes, slides, recorded; imagine a mixed science/ML audience (or the audience for the role in your Week-46 sentence). Accept when: the recording exists and includes at least one slide of honest limitations and one of "what I'd do next".
7. Gate check: verify the End-Phase-4 gate — demoed end-to-end, architecture diagram, limitations, talk recorded — and the course-wide reproducibility bar. Accept when: `GATE.md` checks every item with links, and one flagship derivation (your choice from the rotation) is re-done cold and scanned in.

## Deliverable

The finished capstone repo: code, results, `REPORT.md` (or PDF), demo recording, talk recording, `GATE.md`. Tag `month-12-complete`, write the retro, open the final open-question issue. Course complete.

## Review

1. Cold, on paper: derive one of — PCA from variance maximization, backprop for a 2-layer net, the VAE ELBO, scaled dot-product attention, the DDPM simplified loss, or the policy gradient theorem — whichever the monthly rotation lands on.
2. Week 12→24→36→48: for each capstone, name its single most load-bearing validation check.
3. Week 28: what would you measure to decide whether your capstone's performance is limited by model scale, data, or system design?
4. Week 45: apply pass 1 of the three-pass method to your own capstone report. Does it survive?
5. The course's opening claim was that ML can be learned like physics — derivations first, honest validation, reproducible results. Name one place your capstone falls short of that standard, and what closing that gap would take.
