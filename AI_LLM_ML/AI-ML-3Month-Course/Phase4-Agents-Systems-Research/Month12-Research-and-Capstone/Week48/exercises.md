# Week 48 — Exercises

Work top to bottom — these are the validation week's seven milestones,
instantiated from `PROPOSAL.md`'s evaluation plan and the Week-47 repo. The
system is frozen from E1 onward (lesson §1): bug fixes are logged and force a
full re-run of E1. All the work lives in the capstone repo, the report, and
the recordings per NOTEBOOK_RULES §6; the notebook hosts only the acceptance
checks (results-table completeness, file-exists) and a display cell for E1's
table. `project.md` maps these onto the week's days and carries the per-track
specifics.

## E1 — Final evaluation

Freeze the system, then run the full evaluation plan on it: every
pre-committed metric, every baseline, through the one harness, with multiple
seeds/runs wherever the system is stochastic.
Hint: repeat runs are cheap for sampling/decoding stochasticity — measure
your trial-to-trial spread even at temperature 0 (lesson §2); a margin
smaller than your own spread is not a margin.
Accept when: results table reports every pre-committed metric with variation
across seeds/runs, generated by one command.

## E2 — Stress suite

Design and run ≥5 track-appropriate stress tests drawn from the four
categories of lesson §3 — e.g. copilot: ambiguous and adversarial analysis
questions; fast-sim: energies/angles at the edge of the training range;
repro-extend: sensitivity to the guessed hyperparameters. Record each in
`stress.md`: what was done, what happened, verdict.
Hint: if everything passes, the suite was too gentle — add harder tests until
something breaks; the breaks are the report's limitations section, found
early.
Accept when: `stress.md` reports each test's outcome, including the failures.

## E3 — Ablation

Remove or degrade one core component (per-track choices in lesson §4 and
`project.md`) and re-run the headline metric through the same harness.
Hint: "removing X changes nothing measurable" is a real finding — report it
at full volume; you spent Week 45 distrusting papers that would not.
Accept when: the ablation row is in the results table and one sentence states
what it shows the component contributes.

## E4 — The report

Write the 6–10 page `REPORT.md` (or PDF): intro with the proposal's
contributions marked kept-or-not, architecture diagram, method, results vs.
baselines, limitations, future work, and the "what failed first" subsection
assembled from `BUILDLOG.md`.
Hint: run Week 45's checklist and your own `stress.md` against the draft —
whatever they catch is the limitations section, written before a referee
writes it for you.
Accept when: every claim cites a table/figure, and the limitations section
names ≥3 real weaknesses a referee would find anyway.

## E5 — The demo

Record the screen capture, ≤10 minutes: fresh clone → `uv sync` → one
command → the headline capability, narrated.
Hint: record from the `git clone` onward — the cold start is the entire
proof; if it fails on camera, that is the reproducibility bar failing, which
you need to know today. Fix, log, re-record.
Accept when: the recording shows the clean-clone run, not a warm environment.

## E6 — The mock talk

Build the slides (one point per slide, the title is the point; the report's
figures) and record the 15–20 minute talk for an imagined mixed HEP/ML
audience — each half's terms defined in a line as they appear.
Hint: rehearse once out loud before recording — timing and unsayable
sentences only exist out loud — and write down the two hardest audience
questions first (lesson §6).
Accept when: the recording exists and includes at least one slide of honest
limitations and one of "what I'd do next."

## E7 — Gate check and course close-out

Write `GATE.md` verifying the End-Phase-4 gate (demoed end-to-end,
architecture diagram, limitations, talk recorded) and the course-wide
reproducibility bar, every item linked to its evidence. Re-do one flagship
derivation from the rotation cold, on paper, and scan it in. Then tag
`month-12-complete`, write the 250-word `retro.md`, and open the final
open-question issue.
Hint: an unlinked check is an unchecked check (lesson §7) — and if the
derivation does not come out cold, that goes in the retro's first line, not
in the bin.
Accept when: `GATE.md` checks every item with links, and one flagship
derivation (your choice from the rotation) is re-done cold and scanned in.

## Review

1. Cold, on paper: derive one of — PCA from variance maximization, backprop
   for a 2-layer net, the VAE ELBO, scaled dot-product attention, the DDPM
   simplified loss, or the policy gradient theorem — whichever the monthly
   rotation lands on.
2. (Weeks 12, 24, 36, 48) For each capstone, name its single most
   load-bearing validation check.
3. (Week 28) What would you measure to decide whether your capstone's
   performance is limited by model scale, data, or system design?
4. (Week 45) Apply pass 1 of the three-pass method to your own capstone
   report. Does it survive?
5. The course's opening claim was that ML can be learned like physics —
   derivations first, honest validation, reproducible results. Name one place
   your capstone falls short of that standard, and what closing that gap
   would take.
