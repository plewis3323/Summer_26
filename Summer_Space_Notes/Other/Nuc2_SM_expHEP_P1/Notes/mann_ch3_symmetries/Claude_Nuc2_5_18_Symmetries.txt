[Q1] 2026-05-18

USER: What is invariance and covariance and give example for each?

CLAUDE:

Core distinction:
  INVARIANCE -> a QUANTITY (scalar/number) keeps the SAME VALUE.
  COVARIANCE -> an EQUATION keeps the SAME FORM (both sides transform by the
                same rule, so the law holds in every frame even though the
                individual components change).

1. INVARIANCE
   Q is invariant under transformation T if  Q' = Q.
   The transformation acts but the computed number is unchanged. Invariants
   are scalars built so all frame-dependent pieces cancel.
   Examples:
   - Spacetime interval (Lorentz): s^2 = c^2 t^2 - x^2 - y^2 - z^2. Observers
     disagree on t,x,y,z but compute the SAME s^2.
   - Invariant (rest) mass: m^2 c^4 = E^2 - p^2 c^2. Boost to any frame; E and
     p change, m does not. The workhorse invariant in particle physics
     (invariant-mass spectra -> resonances).
   - Symmetry context (Noether): QED Lagrangian invariant under global phase
     rotation psi -> e^{i alpha} psi  =>  electric charge conserved.

2. COVARIANCE
   Equations are covariant under T if every term transforms by the SAME rule,
   so the equation keeps its form:
       V^mu = W^mu  --T-->  V'^mu = W'^mu,  with V'^mu = Lambda^mu_nu V^nu, etc.
   Components change (V^mu != V'^mu) but the RELATIONSHIP is preserved; the
   physical law looks identical in every frame.
   Examples:
   - Maxwell in tensor form: d_mu F^{mu nu} = mu_0 J^nu. Both sides four-vectors,
     transform identically => same equation in every inertial frame. (E,B form
     is NOT manifestly covariant.)
   - Dirac equation (i gamma^mu d_mu - m) psi = 0 is Lorentz covariant: psi
     transforms via spinor rep, equation keeps form.
   - Newton's F = m a is covariant under spatial rotations (both rotate as
     vectors); NOT covariant under Lorentz boosts -> motivates relativistic
     mechanics.

RELATION: Covariant equations are built from invariants + objects with definite
transformation rules. You write physics covariantly so invariant (physical,
frame-independent) predictions fall out. Mann/Noether framing: a SYMMETRY leaves
the action/Lagrangian INVARIANT; the equations of motion are then COVARIANT; and
Noether's theorem converts the invariance into a conserved charge.

CAVEAT: "covariant" is overloaded. In tensor index language, covariant
(lower index V_mu) vs contravariant (upper index V^mu) is just a
transformation-rule label, NOT the "form-invariance of laws" meaning. In
symmetry discussions the form-invariance meaning is the relevant one.

[Q2] 2026-05-18

USER: does covariant mean the physics stays the same but a transformation on
one frame can change inherit properties of original frame equations

CLAUDE:

Short answer: YES, with one refinement.
  Covariance => the LAW (and the physics it predicts) is the same in every
  frame, but the transformation genuinely changes the COMPONENTS/VALUES of the
  objects in that law.
  Refinement: the pieces that change are NOT artifacts -- they are themselves
  physical, just frame-dependent. What is frame-INDEPENDENT is (a) the FORM of
  the law and (b) the SCALAR INVARIANTS built from it. Covariance is the
  machinery guaranteeing (a) and (b) survive while components reshuffle.

Canonical example: EM field tensor F^{mu nu}.
  Maxwell d_mu F^{mu nu} = mu_0 J^nu is covariant. Boost along x with speed v:
    E_x' = E_x
    E_y' = gamma (E_y - v B_z)
    B_z' = gamma (B_z - (v/c^2) E_y)  ...
  A pure E field (static charge) in one frame is seen as a mix of E and B
  (current -> magnetic field) in a boosted frame. The COMPONENTS E,B differ.
  Preserved:
    1. Form of law: d_mu F^{mu nu} = mu_0 J^nu holds identically in both frames.
    2. Invariants: F_{mu nu}F^{mu nu} ~ (B^2 - E^2/c^2) and
       eps^{mu nu rho sigma}F_{mu nu}F_{rho sigma} ~ E.B  -> same number in
       every frame.
    3. Physical outcome: force on / trajectory of a test charge is the same
       event described consistently in both frames.

Precise restatement:
  - Components (E_y, p^mu, psi, ...)        : CHANGE (frame-dependent),
                                              physical but frame-relative
  - Form of the law (d_mu F^{mu nu}=...)    : UNCHANGED (covariant)
  - Scalar invariants (m^2c^4=E^2-p^2c^2)   : UNCHANGED VALUE (invariant)

So covariant != "everything stays the same." It means the STRUCTURE stays the
same so the frame-independent physics (law + invariants) is protected, while
the frame-dependent description (components) changes in a coordinated way.

Mann Ch.3 point: the magnetic field of a moving charge is not less real than
the electric field of a static charge -- they are the SAME covariant object
F^{mu nu} viewed from two frames. Covariance tells you it is the same physics.

[Q3] 2026-05-18  (Mann pp. 46-47, sec 3.1.1 "Axioms of a Group")

USER: I just read 3.1.1. Jot down important features of this subsection.

CLAUDE:

1. WHY GROUPS -> SYMMETRY
   Symmetry = set of operations/transformations leaving some basic property
   unchanged. Collect them into a set G = {g1,g2,...} (may be infinite). The
   minimal structure needed to "describe a symmetry" is a GROUP.

2. DEFINITION (Table 3.1): set G + binary op "*" with four axioms:
   - CLOSURE:       g1,g2 in G  =>  g1*g2 in G
                    (combo is the same kind of transformation)
   - IDENTITY:      exists I in G with I*gi = gi for all gi
                    (a do-nothing transformation)
   - INVERSE:       every gi has gi^-1 in G, gi*gi^-1 = I
                    (every transformation can be undone)
   - ASSOCIATIVITY: (g1*g2)*g3 = g1*(g2*g3)
                    (result indep. of GROUPING, order unchanged)

3. ORDER MATTERS: in general g1*g2 != g2*g1. If they commute -> ABELIAN.
   - Translations: abelian (order irrelevant).
   - Rotations about a fixed point: non-abelian (x then y != y then x).
   - Associativity lets you re-GROUP but NOT re-ORDER (!= commutativity).

4. TESTING FOR A GROUP: only sure way is to check all four axioms.

5. EXAMPLE - integers Z (Table 3.2):
   - Under addition: a group (id 0, inv -Zi, closed, associative). YES
   - Under multiplication: NOT a group (1/Zi not in Z -> inverse fails). NO
   - Fix: modify the set -> nonzero rationals Q form a group under x.

6. GENERAL PHYSICS LESSON: if a set fails to be a group under an operation,
   often rescue it by modifying the set, the operation, or both. Experimentally
   motivated transformations often ALMOST satisfy the axioms; the theorist's
   job is to intelligently generalize so the desired symmetry holds, then
   exploit it for testable predictions. No general prescription for how.

7. EXAMPLE - U(1) (Table 3.3): unitary group of complex 1x1 matrices,
   G = {e^{i theta}}, op = multiplication, theta in R. All four axioms hold.
   - Continuous parameter theta => UNCOUNTABLY infinite elements
     (contrast: Z under + is COUNTABLY infinite).
   - PHYSICS: U(1) is the symmetry group of electromagnetic interactions
     (Ch. 12).  <-- key forward-looking takeaway.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY: A symmetry is captured by a group (transformations closed
under combination, with identity, inverses, associativity); order generally
matters (abelian vs non-abelian); sets that fail can often be enlarged into
groups; the simplest continuous example U(1) is exactly the symmetry of EM.

[Q5] 2026-05-20  (Mann sec 3.1.3)

USER: so is an irreducible representation a non redundant description of a
group

CLAUDE:

Short answer: yes, basically right -- with a sharper formulation.

WHAT "non-redundant" MEANS PRECISELY:
  A reducible representation can be split into smaller pieces that act
  independently. Concretely, by changing basis you can put ALL the group
  matrices simultaneously into block-diagonal form:

              [ M_g   0  ]
       L_g  = [          ]
              [  0   N_g ]

  Every group element acts on top block and bottom block separately; nothing
  mixes them. So L isn't a new representation, it's just M and N glued
  together. Redundant.

  An IRREDUCIBLE representation (irrep) is one that CANNOT be broken up this
  way -- no change of basis makes all the matrices simultaneously
  block-diagonal. Equivalent (Mann footnote): it has NO INVARIANT SUBSPACE,
  i.e. no proper subspace that the group keeps mapping into itself.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  - Any group has infinitely many representations (just keep gluing).
  - But all of them are built out of the irreps.
  - So the irreps are the ATOMIC, IRREDUCIBLE BUILDING BLOCKS -- the minimal
    complete catalog. Know the irreps -> know every representation.

SMALL CAVEAT ON THE PHRASING:
  An irrep isn't "the non-redundant description of the group" (singular).
  A group usually has SEVERAL irreps of different dimensions. E.g. SU(3) has
  irreps of dim 3, 6, 8, 10, .... The FULL SET of irreps is the non-redundant
  catalog; each individual irrep is one irreducible piece of that catalog.

PHYSICS PAYOFF:
  If a system has symmetry group G, the physically allowed wavefunctions must
  transform under one of G's irreps. That's why quark color wavefunctions
  (SU(3) symmetry) come in dimensions 3, 6, 8, 10, ... and nothing else --
  the math doesn't permit other sizes.

[Q7] 2026-05-20

USER: what is closure rule

CLAUDE:

CLOSURE is one of the four group axioms (Mann sec 3.1.1).
Statement: for a group (G, .), every pair g, h in G satisfies

      g . h  IN  G.

Intuition: the group is "self-contained" under its operation. You can
never combine two members and produce something outside the set.

EXAMPLES:
  Integers under +                       CLOSED   (int + int = int)
  Integers under division                NOT      (1/2 = 0.5 is not an int)
  {0,1,2} under + mod 3                  CLOSED   (sums wrap into {0,1,2})
  Even integers under +                  CLOSED   (even + even = even)
  Odd integers under +                   NOT      (odd + odd = even -- escapes)
  Square rotations {0,90,180,270} under  CLOSED   (any two compose to one of
    composition                                    the four)

HOW TO SPOT IT IN A MULTIPLICATION TABLE:
  Every cell is one of the elements in the headers -- no "new" symbols
  appear inside the grid. In the Z_3 table from the 3.1.4 PDF, every entry
  is 0, 1, or 2. If even one entry were e.g. 5, the set would NOT be
  closed under "+ mod 3" and would not form a group.

WHY IT MATTERS:
  Without closure, the other axioms (associativity, identity, inverses)
  don't even get a chance to apply -- you'd be combining things and
  landing outside the set you're trying to describe. Closure is what
  makes the operation an INTERNAL one.
