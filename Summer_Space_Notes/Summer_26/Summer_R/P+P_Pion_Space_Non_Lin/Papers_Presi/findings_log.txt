   NON-LINEARITY PHYSICS DISCUSSED (two effects added to MC):

     (1) Longitudinal light attenuation in fibers (p.2)
         - Higher-energy showers develop deeper -> longer light path to SiPM
           -> more attenuation.  Shower depth t_max ~ ln(E), dx ~ X0 ln(E),
           exponential attenuation S ~ exp(-dx/Lambda).
         - Result:  S(E)/E  ~  E^(-X0/Lambda).
         - Attenuation length scale  Lambda ~ 1.6 m  (from the TDR).
         - Response change ~ 1.0%, 1.3%, 1.6% at 10, 20, 40 GeV.
         - Implemented in current MC as a z-dependent light efficiency.
         - Not super important for extrapolating non-linearity out to 30 GeV.

     (2) SiPM pixel saturation / occupancy (p.3)
         - N_pix ~ 1.6e5 total pixels (4 SiPMs x 40k each),
           ~400 photoelectrons per GeV.
         - First-order occupancy estimate: 400E/(160000x2) ~ 0.125% x E
           -> roughly 1.25%, 2.5%, 5% at 10, 20, 40 GeV.
         - NOT in the current sPHENIX MC (but was in the test-beam sim);
           now implemented.
         - TWO PARAMETERS for the pixel-occupancy model:
              * Number of electrons per GeV  = 500   (slide value)
              * Number of effective pixels (all pixels)
         - Compared to test-beam results; agreement shown.

     Effect of pixel occupancy on neutral-meson mass (p.4): comparing
     pythia_smear5 vs pythia_smear5_pix, the effect on pi0/eta M_rec/PDG vs pT
     is small (both sit ~1.01-1.02 above PDG over 0-14 GeV).

   SELECTIONS (p.5):
     - Data: ana509, GRL (good-run list).
     - Pythia run 28: main samples MB pythia, plus Eta3 and Eta8.
     - MBD vertex |z| < 40 cm.
     - Cluster cuts: pT(1,2) > 0.25 GeV ; energy asymmetry Asym < 0.6.

   PILEUP INFO (p.6) -- trigger_index 12 and 30:

   VERTEX REWEIGHTING & PILEUP (p.7):
     - MC (MB pythia + EtaX) reweighted to match 0 and 1.5 mrad data.

   *** THIS DOC CONTAINS THE ACTUAL SMEARING PARAMETERS -- FOUND THEM ***

   THE PROBLEM MOTIVATING SMEARING (p.3 "Data pythia comparison"):
     - Pythia sample = MB + jet5 (pT>6 GeV) + jet8 (pT>9 GeV) + jet12 (pT>12 GeV).
     - "Data is much wider than sim..."  i.e. the nominal pythia (pythia_nominal)
       has a NARROWER pi0/eta peak (better resolution) than the real data.
     - "It is unclear why, and thus we are attempting to smear the MC."
     - So "smearing" here = artificially degrading the MC energy/position
       resolution so the simulated pi0/eta peak widths match data.

   DATA-DRIVEN EXTRACTION OF RESOLUTION (p.4):
     - Reference (smearing study slides):
       https://indico.bnl.gov/event/31859/contributions/120756/attachments/68548/117758/EMCal_toyMC_calib_20260302.pdf
     - Method: measure the energy and position resolution in MC and find how
       much ADDITIONAL smearing is needed to reproduce the data.
     - Resolution functional forms shown on the panel (sampling/noise/constant):
         * Energy:   sigma_E / E  =  0.130/sqrt(E)  (+)  0.080/E  (+)  0.080
                     ( the constant term 0.080 was highlighted/boxed )
         * Position: sigma_psi(E) =  0.0055/sqrt(E)  (+)  0.0020
                     ( the 0.0020 term was highlighted/boxed )
       Conditions on that panel: MBD N&S>=1, |v_z| < 20 cm, |eta| < 1.1,
                                 pT > 0.25 GeV, alpha < 0.6, E_scale = f(E)x0.999
     - Lower-panel single-fit examples (pi0 decay photons, MB pythia):
         * Resolution [GeV] = nan/E + 0.1635/sqrt(E) + 0.0000
         * Position (Gaussian width) = 0.0027/sqrt(E) + 0.0039
       (Several candidate parameter sets shown: mean parameter set, global
        minimum parameter set, single-particle-gun response, and examples
        c_e=0.08/c_p=0.002 and c_e=0.05/c_p=0.0025.)

   POST-SMEARING ATTEMPT #1 (p.5):  sample = "pythia_smear2"
     - "This well defined process did not seem to work and the smearing
       factors was insufficient to reproduce the data."  (MC still too narrow.)

   POST-SMEARING ATTEMPT #2 (p.6):  sample = "pythia_smear5"   <== NOMINAL
     - *** Added an ADDITIONAL 8% of energy smearing. ***   <-- key number
     - Agreement in width is at the statistical limit of the MC.
     - Mass is shifted to agree.
     - Large discrepancy remains at high pT in the eta.
     ==> This is the "smear5" sample referenced throughout the nonLin update.

   CALIBRATION (p.7-8):
     - Scale set eta-by-eta (per leading-tower i-eta), 2.0 < pT(pi0) < 5.0 GeV,
       data vs "MB smear5".  M_fit/m_PDG, sigma(pi0)/M(pi0), and Gaussian norm
       all tracked vs leading tower i-eta.
     - Calibration factors FCEMC_calib_ADC_to_ETower: New mean 0.001357
       (Std 0.0003641) vs Old mean 0.001338 (Std 0.0003551), 24576 entries.

   HIGH-pT ETA MESON (p.9):  *** FINAL / TUNED SMEARING PARAMETER SET ***
     (Jaebeom's version of the mu/m_PDG vs pT plot; p+p sqrt(s)=200 GeV)
         * Energy:   sigma_E / E  =  0.180/sqrt(E)  (+)  0.020/E  (+)  0.015
         * Position: sigma_psi(E) =  0.0030/sqrt(E)  (+)  0.0040
         * Energy scale:  E_scale = f(E) x 0.998
       Conditions: MBD N&S>=1, |v_z| < 20 cm, |eta| < 1.1, pT > 0.25 GeV,
                   alpha < 0.6.
     - "Need more stats in MC."  pi0/eta data vs MC mu/m_PDG ~ 1 over 0-14 GeV.

     NOTE: the "(+)" above denotes addition in quadrature (the standard
     a/sqrt(E) (+) b/E (+) c calorimeter resolution parameterization:
     a = stochastic/sampling term, b = noise term, c = constant term).

   CONCLUSION (p.10):
     - Nominal pythia has MUCH better resolution than data.
     - Unclear what the major issue in data is.
     - Extra smearing helps describe the data but needs further tuning.
     - Need to follow up on high-pT eta (only calibration constraint at high pT).

   0mrad vs 1.5mrad (p.11): same as nonLin doc (22%/7% double interactions;

   *** COMPARISON OF ALL SMEARING SETS (p.9) -- defines what each set means ***
     Three additional-smearing variations were tried:
        - smear1  =  "global minimum difference" parameter set
        - smear2  =  constant energy term  c_E = 0.08   (8%)
        - smear3  =  constant energy term  c_E = 0.05   (5%)
     (The position constants pair with these as c_p ~ 0.002 / 0.0025; cf. the
      "Example c_e=0.08/c_p=0.002" and "c_e=0.05/c_p=0.0025" curves on p.6.)
     Findings: stats are limited; NONE of the 3 reproduce the data eta; the pi0
     mass and width show little variation between the sets.

   So the naming convention is: pythia_smearN = nominal pythia with the N-th
   additional-smearing parameter set applied on top of the measured MC
   resolution.  smear5 (and later smear11) are the better-tuned iterations.

 STEP 5 -- Read: PPG12_analysis_note_v4.pdf   (111 pages)
           sPH-ppg-2024-012, v4, dated 2026-05-15
           "Measurement of isolated-prompt photons in p+p at sqrt(s)=200 GeV"
           Authors: Yeonju Go, Shuhang Li, Blair Seidlitz, Justin Bennett,
                    Muhammad Elsayed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   WHAT THE NOTE IS: the formal PPG12 analysis note for the Run-24 p+p
   isolated-prompt-photon cross section (12 < E_T^gamma < 32 GeV, |eta|<0.7,
   truth isolation E_T^iso < 4 GeV in R=0.3). Uses the SAME EMCal + the same
   pi0/eta-derived calibration discussed in the presentations. The MC EMCal
   smearing IS the same machinery as the pythia_smearN samples.

   *** THE DEFINITIVE NUMERICAL SMEARING PARAMETERS (Section 5.2, p.50) ***

   How smearing is applied (Sec 2.2.3, p.11 + Sec 5.2):
     - The EMCal energy resolution in MC is matched to data via an ADDITIVE,
       E_T^gamma-dependent GAUSSIAN smearing applied to the cluster E_T^reco
       at the UNFOLDING response-matrix fill stage (NOT to the ABCD yields,
       photon-ID, or isolation cuts -- those use the unsmeared E_T^reco).
     - Smearing drawn from  N(0, sigma_extra(E)*E)  with
            sigma_extra(E) = sqrt( max(0, sigma_data^2(E) - sigma_MC^2(E)) )
     - Each resolution follows the standard EMCal 3-term form:
            sigma(E)/E = sqrt( p0^2/E + p1^2/E^2 + p2^2 )   (E in GeV)
       i.e. p0 = stochastic/sampling term, p1 = noise term, p2 = constant term.

   THE PARAMETER VALUES (this is what the user asked for):
     - MC intrinsic (fit of UNSMEARED pythia-8 tight+iso prompt-photon response):
            (p0, p1, p2)_MC   = (0.185, 0, 0.040)
     - NOMINAL data target:
            (p0, p1, p2)_data = (0.15, 0.05, 0.05)
            --> gives sigma_extra ~ 0 for E_T <~ 17 GeV, rising to ~2% above
                20 GeV (so smearing only matters at high E_T).
     - Systematic VARIATIONS that bracket the nominal:
            * "no extra smearing":  sigma_data == 0  (sigma_extra == 0)
            * "wider-data":  (p0, p1, p2)_data = (0.13, 0.08, 0.08)
                             --> sigma_extra ~ 6%, approx flat in E_T
       The per-bin max of the two abs. deviations is symmetrized -> "eres"
       systematic; max across 12-32 GeV is +/-9.6% at E_T ~ 27 GeV (Fig 41).

   *** CONNECTION BETWEEN THE PRESENTATIONS AND THE NOTE (important) ***
     - The note's MC term (0.185, 0, 0.040) == the presentations' tuned smear11
       set sigma_E/E = 0.180/sqrt(E) (+) 0.020/E (+) 0.015 region / the
       "single particle gun response" -- i.e. pythia is intrinsically narrow
       (p0~0.185, very small constant 0.040).
     - The note's "wider-data" variation (0.13, 0.08, 0.08) is EXACTLY the
       presentations' data-driven toy-MC extraction
       sigma_E/E = 0.130/sqrt(E) (+) 0.080/E (+) 0.080
       (EMCal_toyMC_calib_20260302.pdf, shown on calib-update slide p.4/6).
     - So the "8%" smearing referenced in the calibration talks = the p0=0.08
       (or p1/p2=0.08) extra term needed to widen the narrow pythia peak to
       match data. The smearN sample naming (smear2/3/5/11) tracks iterations
       of tuning this extra term.

   OTHER RESOLUTION REFERENCE NUMBERS in the note:
     - Test-beam single-photon EMCal resolution (Sec 3.1, p.14):
            sigma_E/E ~= 16%/sqrt(E[GeV]) (+) 5%   [from TDR/test beam, ref 5]
     - Measured reco photon energy resolution (Fig 10, signal pythia, tight+iso):
            sigma(E_T^reco/E_T^truth) falls from ~0.07 at 10 GeV to ~0.05 at
            >25 GeV; energy scale (mean) ~0.99-1.0, dropping to ~0.955 by 45 GeV.
     - Per-bin Gaussian fits (Fig 9): mu ~0.99-0.997, sigma 0.071 (8-10 GeV)
            down to 0.052-0.053 (28-36 GeV).

   ENERGY-SCALE systematic (Sec 5.1): cluster E_T^reco shifted +/-1.1% in MC
     (per Calorimeter Calibration WG) -> up to -10.5%/+12.2%; plus a residual
     non-linearity 1 + s(E_T-10) with s = 5e-4 /GeV (0% at 10 GeV -> +1% at
     30 GeV, from sPHENIX testbeam) -> +/-7.2% max. NOTE the conclusion (p.64)
     flags the +/-1.1% as a PLACEHOLDER pending final Calo Calib guidance.

   SAMPLES / SETUP (for context):
     - Data: Run 24 p+p, runs 47289-53880, production ana521, CDB 2025p007,
       integrated lumi L = 64.37 pb^-1 (+9.13%/-6.75%).
     - MC: pythia-8 (Detroit tune 8.307) + GEANT4; photon5/10/20 + jet8/12/20/30
       (+ matching *_double pileup partners), combined by effective cross section.
     - Two crossing-angle periods split at run 51274:
            0 mrad   runs 47289-51274, L=47 pb^-1, sigma_z~54 cm, 22.4% DI
            1.5 mrad runs 51274-54000, L=17 pb^-1, sigma_z~22 cm,  7.9% DI
     - DI (double-interaction/pileup) blended at cluster-weighted fractions
       f_DI = 0.224 (0 mrad) / 0.079 (1.5 mrad); shifts MBD vertex to the mean
       of the two collisions (Eq z_reco ~ (z1+z2)/2), distorting cluster eta and
       broadening shower shapes -- handled by SI+DI blend + truth-vertex reweight.
     - Photon ID = XGBoost BDT on shower shapes; NPB (non-physics/beam bkg)
       removed by a separate BDT (cut >0.5); purity via data-driven ABCD double
       sideband (0.51 at 10-12 GeV rising to 0.90 above 26 GeV).
     - Unfolding: D'Agostini iterative Bayesian (RooUnfold), N_iter = 2.
     - Result agrees with JETPHOX NLO (CT14nlo) and pythia-8 within
       uncertainties; consistent with PHENIX PRD 86 072008.

   TOTAL SYSTEMATICS (Table 7): per-bin total -30.8%/+31.7% at high E_T,
     dominated by energy resolution (21.2%), energy scale (flat 11.9% + non-lin
     7.3%), purity (tight-ID 6.7%, fit-CI 4.4%, MC-closure 5.8%), MBD vertex
